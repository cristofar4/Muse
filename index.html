    IV

    Anatomy of a scent

    01 / 05

    01

    Top note · the first ten minutes

    Bergamot

    Citrus bergamia

    Cold pressed from the peel. Bright, bitter and green, it is the handshake of a fragrance and the first thing to leave.

    02

    Heart note · the first hours

    Jasmine

    Jasminum sambac

    Picked before sunrise, when the flowers smell strongest. Creamy, sweet, a little animal. The heart is where a perfume tells you who it is.

    03

    Base note · into the evening

    Cedar

    Cedrus atlantica

    Dry, pencil sharp, quietly sweet. Woods hold the lighter notes in place and stay on skin long after the flowers have gone.

    04

    Base note · the glow

    Labdanum

    Cistus ladanifer

    A sticky resin from the rockrose shrub. Warm, leathery, faintly honeyed. It is the reason amber perfumes feel like late sun.

    05

    Base note · the memory

    Frankincense

    Boswellia sacra

    Tears of resin burned for three thousand years. Lemony smoke, cold stone, silence. It makes a fragrance feel older than you are.

      We measure in drops.

      MUSE is a small perfume house. One studio, one perfumer, six signatures, and a stubborn belief that a fragrance should be felt before it is named.

      We make little, slowly.

      We test on skin, never only on paper.

      We refill the same bottle for years.

      We name a scent last.

      I

      How a MUSE is made

      Four stages, roughly eleven weeks, and a great deal of waiting.

      1. 01

        Gather

        Raw materials are chosen harvest by harvest: jasmine picked before dawn, citrus pressed the same week, woods aged before they reach us.

      2. 02

        Compose

        Every accord is weighed by the drop and worn for days. Most ideas never leave the studio. The few that do have earned it.

      3. 03

        Rest

        The blend macerates for eight weeks in the dark, so the notes can settle into one another before anyone smells them again.

      4. 04

        Bottle

        Filled and sealed by hand in heavy glass that is made to be refilled, not replaced.
